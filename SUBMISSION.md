# Assessment 2 — submission map

XR Experience Analysis & Interaction Prototype. This repository is the Unity 6 project for Part 2.

## The interaction being rebuilt

**Direct grab → aim → trigger** on a six-chamber roulette shotgun, from *Devil's Roulette*.
One sentence: *the player picks a shotgun off a table with the controller grip, aims it at a target,
and pulls the trigger on one of six chambers, one of which is live.*

Out of scope on purpose: the original's story, level layout, art, menus, scoring and audio.

## Running it

1. Open with Unity **6000.6.7f1** (OpenXR, XR Interaction Toolkit 3.6.1, URP).
2. Open `Assets/Scenes/ThePit.unity`.
3. Press Play. The XR Device Simulator starts automatically in the Editor:
   right mouse to look, `Y` to drive the right controller, `G` to grip, left click to fire.

## Building for the Quest

```
Unity.exe -batchmode -quit -projectPath <project> -buildTarget Android ^
  -executeMethod PitAndroidBuilder.BuildApk -logFile <log>
```

Output: `Builds/ThePit.apk` — arm64-v8a, `minSdk 29`, `targetSdk 34`, debug-signed,
package `com.tus.devilsroulette.thepit`. Install with `adb install -r`, launch with
`adb shell am start -n com.tus.devilsroulette.thepit/com.unity3d.player.UnityPlayerGameActivity`.

## Design rationale (HCI)

- **Affordance and signifiers.** The gun rests on a felt inlay directly under the work lamp, so the
  object to interact with is the brightest thing at hand. A hover highlight confirms the controller is
  aimed at a grabbable object before the player commits.
- **Mapping.** One physical action per outcome: grip holds, trigger fires. No ray, no menu.
- **Feedback (gulf of evaluation).** Every shot resolves visibly (ring flash, bullet mark), audibly
  (shot or dry click, impact clank) and haptically (0.8 live / 0.25 empty), and the placard restates the
  chamber count and hit count in world space. The player never has to guess what happened.
- **Comfort.** No locomotion, so no vection-induced sickness. The table is at 0.83 m, inside the
  comfortable reach envelope for a standing player, and recoil is expressed as a short gun kick plus a
  haptic pulse rather than camera shake, which keeps the head stable.
- **Error recovery.** There is no walk-around, so a gun dropped under the table would end the session;
  it is returned to its resting pose after 2.5 s out of reach.
- **Accessibility.** Either controller can grab and fire; the feedback is redundant across sight, sound
  and touch, so a player who cannot hear the click still sees the placard, and one who cannot see the
  small text still hears the shot.

## Credits

All geometry, materials, textures and audio in the scene are generated by this project's own scripts.
No third-party art assets are included. The video overlays use Segoe UI and Microsoft YaHei, which are
system fonts.

## Known limitations

- Not yet run on a headset; the build has only been verified as an APK (signature, ABI, manifest).
- Feedback timings are tuned by eye in the Editor, not by testing on device.
- English-only placard text; no hand-tracking input; no comfort or height options yet.
